Company Snapshot

Infosys is India's 2nd-largest IT services company (behind TCS), providing consulting, technology, outsourcing and digital transformation services across North America, Europe and India. Core offerings span cloud, data & AI (including its generative-AI platform "Topaz"), enterprise applications (Finacle, Panaya), cybersecurity, and application modernization. Valuation vs history & peers

At a P/E of ~15x, Infosys trades noticeably below its own 5-year average band (historically ~22–28x) and below large-cap IT peers like TCS (~23x, per Nov 2025 reporting) and HCLTech (~25x). On this basis the stock screens as Cheap relative to its own history and to peers — but a de-rating like this can also reflect genuine growth-rate compression (constant-currency revenue growth has slowed industry-wide) rather than a pure mispricing, so it should not be read as an automatic signal.

Verdict (per interpretation rules): Cheap vs 5Y average and vs peer median — driven mainly by the ~19% one-year share price fall rather than earnings deterioration.

Overall View

Strong
Fundamental Quality — consistent high ROE/ROCE, low leverage, strong free cash flow, and a valuation that currently sits below its own historical average and peer median.

Key strengths

  • Decade-long track record of ROE/ROCE well above 15%, with minimal debt
  • Strong and improving free cash flow generation (₹31,259 Cr TTM-adjacent FY26)
  • Currently valued below its 5-year average P/E and below large-cap peers

Key watch-points

  • Revenue growth has moderated (3Y CAGR of 7% vs 10Y average of 11%) — reflects sector-wide demand softness
  • Stock price has fallen ~19% over the past year despite steady earnings — market appears cautious on near-term demand/AI-disruption narratives for IT services
  • Debtor days have crept up slightly; worth tracking working-capital discipline in coming quarters

Metric to track next

Quarterly constant-currency revenue growth and large-deal TCV (total contract value) — these are the clearest early signals of whether demand is stabilising or continuing to soften, and will likely drive re-rating (or further de-rating) of the stock.
